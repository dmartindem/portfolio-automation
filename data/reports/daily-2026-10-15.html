Recommendations

Everything above this line is measured or sourced. Everything below it is interpretation. It is not financial advice, it has no view of your tax situation or anything outside this portfolio, and it can be wrong.

1. Point this month's contributions away from large-cap growth. Confidence: high. New money is the only lever that reshapes concentration at zero tax cost, and concentration is precisely what this morning's tape is testing — mega-cap 8 at 50.4% look-through, semis at 22.77%, and five growth funds that concentrate rather than diversify. Directing new contributions to broad total-market, ex-US or short-duration treasuries moves the ratio without realising a cent of gain. Counterargument: contributions are small against a core that is roughly 90% of the account, so this shifts concentration by fractions of a point per month — slow enough that it is not a hedge against an event two weeks out; and if the AI complex keeps compounding, steering new money out of it carries real opportunity cost. Tax: none — no sale.

2. Decide the ASTS Jan-2027 calls rather than letting Friday pass. Confidence: high that a decision is due; no view on direction. The $25/$30/$35 strikes are deep ITM at 1.40% of the account and expire 15 Jan 2027. Roll, convert to stock, or ride to expiry all remain open now and get more expensive to choose late. Counterargument: deep ITM LEAPs with 90 days left hold little time premium to protect, so the 90-DTE rule implies more urgency than the position does — and rolling pays spread to swap a winner for a worse strike. Tax: closing or exercising realises gain in this tax year. For reference, the ASTS stock position's gain share of proceeds is 26.9% — among the cheapest in the account to touch — but these calls carry far larger percentage gains, so the realised share is higher. And since your short options are invisible to this export, any roll has to be priced against your actual full ladder, not this brief.

3. Close the four decayed Jan-2027 calls (AUR, ACHR, QS, SIRI) before 31 Dec. Confidence: high. Combined 0.04% of the account, near-certain total losses, and in the same expiry as item 2 — one session, two jobs. Tax: the friction runs the other way here; selling realises a deductible loss, not a gain. Counterargument: the position is small enough that the benefit may be immaterial, and spreads on near-worthless options can consume most of it.

4. Do not trim NVDA into this selloff. Confidence: medium-high. The instinct on an AI-financing scare is to cut the 15.05% all-in exposure, but gain share of proceeds is 79.2% — roughly four-fifths of any sale is taxable gain and selling realises it. Earnings are 18 Nov (estimated), which makes this a hold-through event unless the thesis itself changes. Counterargument: tax friction is not a thesis. If the AI capex cycle is genuinely credit-financed and that credit is repricing — CoreWeave bonds over 15% is the live evidence — then even a 79% tax cost can beat a structural de-rating. Hold here is a position, not a default.
