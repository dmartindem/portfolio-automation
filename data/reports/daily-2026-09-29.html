Recommendations

Everything above this line is measured or sourced. Everything below it is interpretation. It is not financial advice, it has no view of your tax situation or anything outside this portfolio, and it can be wrong.

1. Direct new monthly contributions away from the large-cap growth funds. Confidence: high.
VUG, VOOG, QQQ, QQQM and IUSG are 44.7% of the account and their top holdings are the same names held directly — they add 5.54pp to NVDA and 4.42pp to AAPL on a look-through basis, so they concentrate rather than diversify. New money is the only lever that reshapes concentration at zero tax cost. Trimming NVDA instead would make 79.2% of proceeds taxable gain, and selling realises it.
Counterargument: that overlap is precisely what has driven returns; deliberately buying lower-expected-return diversifiers is a real cost, and redirecting contributions moves a 15% position only over years, not quarters.

2. Hold NVDA. Do not trim on the buyback. Confidence: medium.
$235 billion of remaining authorization through fiscal 2028 is a persistent bid and a statement of management confidence in cash generation. It is not a reason to add at 15.05% all-in, and not a reason to sell into it either — a trim realises 79.2% of proceeds as gain.
Counterargument: buyback announcements are among the weakest signals available and tend to cluster at peak cash generation. The authorization does nothing about the fact that a single name is 15% of the book; if the concentration is the worry, this news is irrelevant to it.

3. ASTS: watch the change-of-control plan, act on nothing. Confidence: low on interpretation, high on the mechanics.
The plan itself is standard. What makes it worth a line is that you write calls and puts on ASTS: a takeover gap is the worst outcome for a short call, so any rise in transaction probability, however small, is asymmetric for that sleeve rather than for the stock. The 17 Oct escalation date on the Jan-2027 calls is the natural point to review the whole options position. Note the Fidelity export has no Quantity column, so written positions are invisible to this brief — treat the options picture here as incomplete.
Counterargument: severance plans of this shape are boilerplate, frequently adopted years before any transaction and usually before none at all. Reading deal intent into one is pattern-matching, and restructuring an options book on it would be acting on noise.
Tax: no sale contemplated. Separately, the three Jan-2028 ASTS calls hold 58% of the harvestable loss inventory, but wash-sale treatment under §1091 for options while long the stock is genuinely unsettled — that is a question for your tax preparer, not for this report.

4. AMD and GS: no action. The World Labs acquisition is strategically interesting at 2.36% all-in but not position-changing. GS succession is a governance watch item at 2.14%; a trim there would make 70.7% of proceeds taxable gain, which is a poor trade for a CEO headline.
