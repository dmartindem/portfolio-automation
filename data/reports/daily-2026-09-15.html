Recommendations

Everything above this line is measured or sourced. Everything below it is interpretation. It is not financial advice, it has no view of your tax situation or anything outside this portfolio, and it can be wrong.

1. Route new contributions away from large-cap growth. Confidence: moderate-high.
This is the only lever that reshapes concentration at zero tax cost. NVDA is 15.1% all-in and the most expensive position in the account to touch — 79.2% of any NVDA sale proceeds is taxable gain, and selling realises it. Contributions do the same work for free.
Counterargument: concentration in exactly these names is what produced the current gain; diluting reduces expected return, not just risk. And adding to the index core does not diversify — VUG/VOOG/QQQ/IUSG are the same companies. Only a genuinely different sleeve (ex-US, value, small-cap, short-duration bonds) moves the number.

2. Do not pre-position for the FOMC. Confidence: moderate-high.
The hike is largely priced; the variance is in the dot plot, not the decision. Trading a priced event is a coin flip with a tax bill attached.
Counterargument: "priced in" is about direction, not magnitude — a hawkish dot plot could hurt a 50% mega-cap book badly, and anyone who would capitulate after a drawdown is better off reducing before one.
Tax: any pre-emptive trim realises gain — NVDA 79.2% of proceeds, GS 70.7%, AAPL 59.8%.

3. Close the four decayed Jan-2027 calls before 31 Dec. Confidence: moderate.
AUR $7, ACHR $22, QS $32, SIRI $35 are near-certain total losses. They expire 15 Jan 2027 — letting them lapse pushes the loss into tax year 2027. Closing before year-end books it in 2026.
Counterargument: the amounts are trivial against the account and the commissions may eat the benefit; deferring a loss is not a disaster.
Tax: these are losses — no gain realised. Separately, the three ASTS Jan-2028 calls hold 58% of the harvestable loss inventory, but you are long ASTS stock and the Jan-2027 calls, so §1091 wash-sale treatment there is genuinely unsettled. That one belongs with your tax preparer, not this report.
